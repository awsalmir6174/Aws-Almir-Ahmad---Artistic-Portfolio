Under instruction, 2013 to 2018

Trained by Engineer Rawiah Harba in Saudi Arabia. Eight archived works survive from this period, between March 2013 and July 2015. Work carried on to 2018 at roughly one project every three to four months, but those pieces were never photographed and most are no longer accessible.

2013

Syrian Children’s Choir, January 2013 to January 2015

A choir assembled in Saudi Arabia after the outbreak of the war in Syria, bringing displaced Syrian children together around song, poetry and art. Weekly rehearsals of about three hours, October through April, paused each summer. Six recorded productions are listed further down this page.

Paused, 2018 to 2020

No work was made during this period.

Independent, 2021 to 2025

Work resumed in January 2021 without instruction. Eight archived works follow. Output held steady through 2021 and 2022, then slowed after the move to Canada in July 2022, where the practice continues alongside university, work and competitive wrestling. Aws and Rawiah Harba still meet once or twice every six months to go over current projects.

What this archive does not hold

Pottery, clay work and copper tooling were a regular part of the training between 2013 and 2018. None of it is accessible now, and none of it is counted in the hours above. Drawings and paintings made between 2016 and 2018 were not photographed either. The sixteen works on this page are the surviving record, not the full output.

Verification

Engineer Rawiah Harba taught Aws visual art from 2013 and served as assistant lead of the Syrian Children’s Choir. She can confirm the instruction period, the choir dates and rehearsal pattern, the six productions, and the independent work since 2021. Her contact details are supplied directly to admissions offices and are not published here.
